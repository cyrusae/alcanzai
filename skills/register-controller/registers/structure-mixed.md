# Sentence Structure: Mixed (default)

Professional but readable. Moderate sentence complexity. No contractions, but no dense academic prose either. The middle ground between coffee chat and dissertation chapter.

**Philosophy**: Precise and clear without being formal or casual. Suitable for notes you'd share with peers or re-read yourself during active research.

## Core rules

1. **No contractions**: "it is", "they are", "cannot" — but avoid sounding stiff.

2. **Active voice preferred, passive acceptable**: "The researchers analyzed..." preferred over "The data were analyzed...", but passive is fine when it reads naturally.

3. **Medium sentence length**: Average 15-25 words. Some short sentences for emphasis, some longer ones for complex ideas — but no run-ons.

4. **Mixed transitions**: Can use both direct ("Also", "And") and formal ("Additionally", "However", "This suggests") — choose what reads most clearly.

5. **Third person or neutral**: Avoid "I" or "you" unless in the why_you_cared section where second person ("you") is appropriate.

## Examples

**ML (Mixed)**: "The transformer architecture processes all input tokens simultaneously using multi-head self-attention, rather than sequentially as in RNNs. This parallel approach substantially reduces training time while also improving the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies. On standard benchmarks, the architecture outperformed previous state-of-the-art by 2.1 BLEU points."

**Sociolinguistics (Mixed)**: "The corpus comprises 40 sociolinguistic interviews following Labovian methodology, designed to elicit natural speech across multiple style contexts. Analysis reveals significant age stratification in phonological variation, with younger speakers consistently leading in the adoption of innovative features. This pattern suggests active language change in progress rather than age-graded variation."

## Watch for

- Mixed is the DEFAULT register for most alcanzai synthesis
- Professional without being stuffy; readable without being chatty
- Appropriate for notes you'd share with a supervisor or reading group
