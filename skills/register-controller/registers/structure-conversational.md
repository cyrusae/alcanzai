# Sentence Structure: Conversational

Natural spoken-style prose. Contractions, active voice, shorter sentences. Like explaining the paper over coffee, not presenting at a conference.

## Core rules

1. **Use contractions freely**: "it's", "they're", "can't", "there's" — these improve flow without losing clarity.

2. **Active voice as default**: "The researchers trained the model on 40GB of text." Not "The model was trained on 40GB of text."

3. **Shorter sentences**: One idea per sentence. If a sentence has more than 2 commas or any semicolons, break it up.
   - Good: "The model processes all positions at once. This parallel approach is much faster than RNNs. It also captures long-range dependencies better."
   - Bad: "The model processes all positions simultaneously, which represents a parallel approach that is substantially faster than RNNs while also improving the capture of long-range dependencies."

4. **Direct transitions**: "So", "Also", "The key point:", "In other words:" — not "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Consequently".

5. **Optional discourse markers** (use when they genuinely help, skip when not):
   - "Here's the thing...", "Basically...", "It turns out..."
   - Don't add these just for tone — prioritize efficiency

## Examples

**ML (Conversational)**: "Here's what makes this model different: it processes all the words at once instead of going left-to-right. That's way faster for training. The trick is the attention mechanism. Each word 'looks at' all the other words and figures out which ones matter most."

**Sociolinguistics (Conversational)**: "They interviewed 40 working-class speakers in natural conversation settings. The goal was to capture how people actually talk, not how they talk when they know they're being recorded—though that's always a bit of a challenge."

## Watch for

- Conversational ≠ sloppy or imprecise; it just means clear and direct
- Accurate and readable, not dumbed down
- Varies sentence length: mix short punchy sentences with medium explanations
