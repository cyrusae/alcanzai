# Jargon Density: Selective (default)

Use technical terminology as primary language. Gloss specialized terms inline on first use. Trust reader to remember after that.

**Philosophy**: Technical terms are the right words—don't avoid them, but don't assume familiarity with specialized ones. Gloss builds vocabulary through exposure.

## Core rules

1. **Technical terms as primary language**: "The study analyzes alveolar fricatives in word-final position." (Then gloss if needed.)

2. **Inline glosses for specialized concepts**: `technical-term (accessible explanation)`
   - "multi-head self-attention (parallel attention mechanisms focusing on different word relationships)"
   - "/æ/-tensing (when the vowel in 'cat' becomes tighter and higher)"

3. **What to gloss**: Specialized terminology requiring domain-specific coursework. Abbreviations on first use.

4. **What NOT to gloss**: General academic vocabulary (hypothesis, methodology, corpus, analysis). Terms already glossed. Well-known abbreviations after expansion.

5. **Expand acronyms once**: Full name + brief explanation, then use acronym freely.
   - "VOT (Voice Onset Time—delay between consonant release and vowel start)"

## Examples

**ML (Selective)**: "The transformer employs multi-head self-attention (parallel attention mechanisms that focus on different aspects of input relationships) with positional encoding (markers indicating word position). Unlike RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks—models that process sequences step by step), transformers process all tokens simultaneously."

**Stats (Selective)**: "Results showed p < 0.05 (less than 5% chance the pattern is random), with effect sizes d = 0.3 to 0.8 (Cohen's d, where 0.3 is small-medium, 0.8 is large). The 95% confidence intervals suggest a substantial population effect."

## Watch for

- This is the DEFAULT register for most alcanzai use cases
- Gloss once, use freely after that
- Glosses should be brief (one phrase), not mini-essays
