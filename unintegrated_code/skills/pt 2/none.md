# Jargon Density: None

## Strategy
Prioritize accessible explanations over technical terminology. Use plain language as primary, with technical terms introduced secondarily for vocabulary building.

**Philosophy**: Explain the concept first in familiar terms, then introduce the technical term as "what experts call this." Reader learns vocabulary without it being a barrier to understanding.

---

## When to Use This Register

✅ **Use None Jargon when:**
- Writing for smart non-experts (e.g., partner-shareable notes)
- Reader is new to the field but intellectually capable
- Goal is broad understanding before technical precision
- Recovering from burnout/hiatus and need gentle re-entry
- Creating notes for interdisciplinary audiences

❌ **Don't use None Jargon when:**
- Reader is actively working in the field
- Speed and efficiency matter more than accessibility
- Technical precision is critical to understanding
- Writing for expert literature review

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Accessible Explanation First
Lead with plain language description that conveys the concept clearly.

**Bad**: "The study examines VOT in word-initial stops."
**Good**: "The study looks at the tiny delay between releasing a consonant sound and starting the vowel—what linguists call Voice Onset Time (VOT)—specifically in sounds like 'p' and 't' at the start of words."

### 2. Technical Term as Appositive
Introduce jargon after explanation, in parentheses or appositive position.

**Pattern**: `[Plain explanation] ([technical term])`

**Examples**:
- "the hissing sound made with your tongue near your tooth ridge, like 's' or 'z' (alveolar fricative)"
- "switching between languages mid-conversation (code-switching)"
- "when vowel sounds shift in predictable patterns across a region (vowel chain shift)"

### 3. Expand All Acronyms on First Use
Never assume acronym familiarity. Always spell out with gloss.

**Examples**:
- "VOT (Voice Onset Time—the delay between releasing a consonant and starting the vowel)"
- "RNN (Recurrent Neural Network—a type of AI that processes sequences one step at a time)"
- "ANOVA (Analysis of Variance—a statistical test comparing multiple groups)"

### 4. Use Familiar Analogies (When Helpful)
Connect to everyday experience when it genuinely clarifies, but don't force analogies where technical explanation is clearer.

**Examples**:
- "Think of attention mechanisms like a spotlight—the model focuses on relevant parts of the input while dimming the rest."
- "Multivariate regression is like untangling multiple factors that all influence an outcome, similar to figuring out whether sleep, stress, or caffeine most affects your focus."
- "Hegemony is cultural dominance that feels so natural you don't notice it—like how we assume 'dinner time' means evening, even though that's culturally specific."

## Examples Across Domains

### Linguistics/Phonetics
**Technical**: "Participants exhibited /æ/-tensing in pre-nasal environments."
**None Jargon**: "When speakers said words with the 'a' sound (like in 'cat') followed by nose sounds like 'n' or 'm', that vowel became tighter and higher in the mouth (/æ/-tensing)."

### Machine Learning
**Technical**: "The transformer architecture employs multi-head self-attention with positional encoding."
**None Jargon**: "Instead of processing words one by one, the model looks at all words simultaneously and learns which ones are most relevant to each other. Since word order matters ('dog bites man' vs 'man bites dog'), the system adds position markers to track where each word appears—an approach called multi-head self-attention with positional encoding."

### Sociolinguistics
**Technical**: "The corpus comprises 40 sociolinguistic interviews from working-class informants."
**None Jargon**: "The data set contains 40 recorded conversations with working-class speakers, conducted in a relaxed style designed to capture natural speech patterns (sociolinguistic interviews)."

### Statistics
**Technical**: "Results showed p < 0.05 with 95% confidence intervals."
**None Jargon**: "The pattern was statistically significant, meaning there's less than a 5% chance it happened by random coincidence (p < 0.05). The researchers can be 95% confident the true effect falls within the measured range."

### Critical Theory
**Technical**: "The text exemplifies epistemic violence through hegemonic discourse."
**None Jargon**: "The text does harm by treating one way of knowing as the only legitimate way—excluding other perspectives so thoroughly they become invisible. This is what scholars call epistemic violence within hegemonic (culturally dominant) ways of talking about the topic."

## Integration with Output Tasks

### Quick Summaries
- Lead with accessible overview
- Introduce 2-3 key technical terms as appositives
- Expand all acronyms on first use
- Use analogies when they genuinely clarify

### Detailed Summaries
- Accessible explanation for each new concept per section
- Build technical vocabulary progressively (term introduced in one section used in later ones)
- Analogies where helpful, especially for complex mechanisms
- Track which terms have been introduced

### Glossary Extraction
- Start with accessible definitions
- Add technical term as "Also known as: [term]"
- Include example sentences using plain language
- Cross-reference related concepts

---

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing synthesis in None Jargon register, verify:

- [ ] No unexplained technical terms in first mention
- [ ] All acronyms expanded on first use with brief gloss
- [ ] Key concepts explained in plain language before introducing jargon
- [ ] Analogies used where they genuinely clarify (not forced)
- [ ] Technical terms positioned as secondary to explanation, not primary language
- [ ] A smart 15-year-old could follow the main argument
- [ ] Vocabulary building happens through exposure, not assumption

---

## Common Pitfalls

❌ **Too much glossing**: Don't interrupt flow with constant asides
**Fix**: Gloss only on first use, trust reader to remember

❌ **Condescending tone**: "Simply put..." or "In layman's terms..."
**Fix**: Write naturally, as if explaining to curious peer

❌ **Losing precision**: Over-simplifying to point of inaccuracy
**Fix**: Accessible doesn't mean imprecise—check that plain language preserves meaning

❌ **Forgetting vocabulary building**: Pure plain language with no technical terms
**Fix**: Introduce terms as appositives so reader builds field literacy

---

## Notes for Implementation

- **Default register for**: Partner-shareable notes, burnout recovery, new field entry
- **Word count**: Longest register (explanations add length)
- **Tone**: Warm but not condescending, clear but not oversimplified
- **Pacing**: Slower, more patient—reader is learning while reading

**Remember**: None Jargon isn't "dumbed down"—it's optimized for learning. A smart reader unfamiliar with the field should finish understanding both the concepts AND the vocabulary used to discuss them.
