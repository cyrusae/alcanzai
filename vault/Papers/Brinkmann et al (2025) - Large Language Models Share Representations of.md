# Large Language Models Share Representations of Latent Grammatical Concepts Across Typologically Diverse Languages

**Brinkmann, Jannik et al.** • 2025

> [!quote] Memorable Quote
> "The internal lingua franca of large language models may not be English words per se, but rather concepts."

## Quick Refresh

This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) share internal representations of grammatical concepts—like number, gender, and tense—across different languages. Using sparse autoencoders (SAEs—a technique that decomposes neural networks into interpretable feature directions), the researchers trained models on Llama-3-8B and Aya-23-8B and found that up to 50% of features encoding the same morphosyntactic concept overlap across typologically diverse languages. They validated these shared representations through causal interventions, demonstrating that features can be activated to reliably change model predictions across languages, and showed that models rely heavily on these multilingual features rather than language-specific ones.

## Why You Cared

You care about this because it explains a puzzling empirical puzzle: why English-dominant models (&gt;90% English training data) perform so well on non-English languages. The paper provides mechanistic evidence that models learn language-independent abstract grammatical concepts rather than memorizing language-specific patterns—a finding with implications for both model understanding and downstream tasks like machine translation and multilingual prompt tuning. This work also demonstrates a rigorous methodology for causal interpretability in neural networks that extends beyond single probes to feature-level interventions with clear behavioral effects.

## Abstract

Human bilinguals often use similar brain regions to process multiple languages, depending on when they learned their second language and their proficiency. In large language models (LLMs), how are multiple languages learned and encoded? In this work, we explore the extent to which LLMs share representations of morphsyntactic concepts such as grammatical number, gender, and tense across languages. We train sparse autoencoders on Llama-3-8B and Aya-23-8B, and demonstrate that abstract grammatical concepts are often encoded in feature directions shared across many languages. We use causal interventions to verify the multilingual nature of these representations; specifically, we show that ablating only multilingual features decreases classifier performance to near-chance across languages. We then use these features to precisely modify model behavior in a machine translation task; this demonstrates both the generality and selectivity of these feature's roles in the network. Our findings suggest that even models trained predominantly on English data can develop robust, cross-lingual abstractions of morphosyntactic concepts.

We release code, data, and autoencoders at github.com/ jannik-brinkmann/multilingual-features.
