# Discovering Differences in Strategic Behavior between Humans and LLMs

**Wang, Caroline et al.** • 2026

> [!quote] Memorable Quote
> "Frontier LLMs employ substantially larger opponent models, suggesting that the superior win rates of Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro and GPT 5.1 stems from maintaining more complex models of opponent behavior than humans."

## Quick Refresh

This paper uses AlphaEvolve, an automated program discovery tool, to generate interpretable behavioral models from human and LLM gameplay data in Iterated Rock-Paper-Scissors (IRPS). Rather than imposing human-designed models, the system discovers programs that fit behavioral data while remaining interpretable as Python functions grounded in game theory concepts. The analysis reveals that frontier LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, GPT 5.1) outperform humans at higher rates and converge to exploitation strategies more rapidly, but achieved similar win rates at convergence. The structural explanation: frontier LLMs maintain more sophisticated opponent models—tracking conditional frequencies (3×3 or 3×3×3 matrices) compared to humans' simpler move-frequency tracking—enabling more effective strategic adaptation.

## Why You Cared

You care about this because it addresses a critical gap: while prior work studies LLM strategic capabilities in isolation, this paper does direct structural comparison of human versus LLM behavior without forcing human-centric hypotheses onto LLMs. The use of automated model discovery rather than hand-designed equations is methodologically novel for behavioral game theory. More practically, if you are evaluating LLMs as digital twins for human behavior or researching strategic reasoning in AI, this paper provides concrete evidence that frontier LLMs are non-equivalent proxies—they solve the same games differently through internal mechanisms that resist simple human explanation. The interpretable programs also provide a model-agnostic way to monitor behavioral capabilities in strategic contexts, useful for alignment work.

## Abstract

As Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in social and strategic scenarios, it becomes critical to understand where and why their behavior diverges from that of humans. While behavioral game theory (BGT) provides a framework for analyzing behavior, existing models do not fully capture the idiosyncratic behavior of humans or black-box, non-human agents like LLMs. We employ AlphaEvolve, a cutting-edge program discovery tool, to directly discover interpretable models of human and LLM behavior from data, thereby enabling open-ended discovery of structural factors driving human and LLM behavior. Our analysis on iterated rock-paper-scissors reveals that frontier LLMs can be capable of deeper strategic behavior than humans. These results provide a foundation for understanding structural differences driving differences in human and LLM behavior in strategic interactions.
